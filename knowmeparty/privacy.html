Squadoo

Privacy Policy

Last updated: 25 October 2026

In short: Squadoo has no server and no accounts. In Who Knows Who, your name and answers travel only between the phones in your room and are not stored after the game. Quick Three runs on one phone and sends nothing anywhere. The developer never sees what you type or answer. The one outside service the app talks to is RevenueCat, which handles in-app purchases and keeps a few anonymous counters.

What you enter

  • Your player name and colour. You choose them on first launch and can change them any time.
  • Your answers during a game. In Who Knows Who: answers about yourself and guesses about the other players. In Quick Three: the team count, the game settings you pick and the team scores.
  • A few choices in onboarding. Who usually plays and what you like (for example food, movies, sports). The app uses them only to suggest packs.
  • Whether you are 18 or older. If you tap "I'm 18+" to see the adult packs, the app remembers that on your phone. It is not checked against anything.

That is all. The app does not ask for your email, phone number, contacts, photos, location or microphone.

Where it is stored

Your name, colour, chosen language, sound and music settings, onboarding choices, the 18+ choice, the Quick Three settings, the list of questions and cards this phone has already shown, and your collection of titles are saved only on your phone, in the app's own storage. For some titles (for example "Old Friends") the app remembers the first names of the people you played with and how many games you played together, also only on your phone. Answers are kept in memory for the length of a game and are never written to disk.

If iPhone backup is turned on, the app's small settings file is included in your backup to iCloud or your computer, like any other app's settings. Apple operates those backups; the developer has no access to them.

Playing with other people (Who Knows Who)

The game connects phones in the same room directly to each other over Wi-Fi and Bluetooth, using Apple's Multipeer Connectivity. Nothing passes through the internet or any server. The connection is encrypted.

To play, the phones exchange what the game needs: each player's name and colour, the game's language and the answers given during the round. Other players in your room will see your name and your answers; that is the game. When the game ends, the other phones do not keep them.

iOS will ask you for Local Network access (and may mention Bluetooth). It is used only to find the other phones in the room. Quick Three does not use it.

When data leaves your phone because you chose to

You can share an invitation to the app (a link and a QR code), a picture of the results, a title you earned or a "knows me" score using the standard iOS share sheet. These pictures show the first names that were in the game. You pick where it goes: Messages, a chat app, social media. Once sent, that service's own rules apply.

Writing to us. "Write to us" in Settings opens your own mail app with a draft addressed to us. Below your message the draft already contains the app version, iOS version, iPhone model and the app's language, so a bug can be reproduced. You can read or delete those lines before sending. If you send it, we receive your email address and what you wrote, and use them only to reply. We do not add you to any list, and you can ask us to delete the conversation at any time.

Rating the app. The rating prompt and the review page are Apple's own. What you write there goes to Apple and the App Store, not through the app.

Purchases

Payments are processed by Apple. The developer never receives your name, Apple Account, card or payment details.

To know which packs you have unlocked, the app uses RevenueCat. RevenueCat receives a random anonymous ID created by the app, the details of your purchases (which product, when, in which country's store, the subscription status) and basic technical details such as the app version and iOS version. Like any online service, it sees the IP address of the request. It does not receive your name, email, answers or anything you type in the app.

Anonymous counters

To understand how the app is used, it keeps a few numbers on your phone and sends them to RevenueCat, under the same anonymous ID used for purchases:

  • how many games this phone has finished, in total and for each of the two games, and how many of them it hosted;
  • how many of those games had three or more players, and the largest group so far;
  • how many players were in the last game and, on the host's phone in Who Knows Who, which question pack or mode it used;
  • where the purchase screen was shown (for example after onboarding, after the first game, or from Settings) and how many times, and which product was bought last and from which screen.

That is the whole list. No names, no answers, no question or card text, nothing typed in the app. The numbers are not linked to your identity and are not used for advertising.

No ads or tracking

The app shows no ads, contains no third-party analytics or crash-reporting tools, does not track you across other apps or websites and does not access your device's advertising identifier.

Children

Squadoo is not directed at children under 13, and the developer does not knowingly collect data from them. As described above, the app itself collects no personal information from anyone. The packs marked 18+ are hidden until a player says they are 18 or older. That is a self-declared choice, not an identity check.

Deleting your data

  • On your phone: delete the app and everything it saved is removed. Copies inside an older iPhone backup stay there until that backup is replaced or deleted.
  • At RevenueCat: email us and we will ask RevenueCat to delete the record for your anonymous ID, including the counters. Your purchase history with Apple itself stays with Apple.
  • Things you shared: pictures or invitations you sent to other apps are controlled by those apps.

Changes to this policy

If a future version starts handling data differently, this page will be updated before that version is released, and the date at the top will change.
